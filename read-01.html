What even is El Niño?

El Niño is when the surface of the central and eastern tropical Pacific Ocean stays warmer than usual for months at a time. That patch of warm water is huge, so it nudges weather patterns around the world, including ours.

It is the warm half of a natural see-saw called ENSO, short for El Niño–Southern Oscillation. The cool half is La Niña, when that same stretch of ocean is cooler than usual. Between the two is a "neutral" state.

El Niño does not come on a fixed schedule. Episodes return irregularly, roughly every two to seven years, and a typical one lasts about nine months to a year.

The name comes from fishers in Peru. They noticed warm water arriving off their coast around Christmas and named it after the Christ child, El Niño, Spanish for "the boy."

KEY IDEA

El Niño isn't a storm. It's a months-long warm spell in the ocean.

Quick check: warm phase = El Niño. Cool phase = ______?

La Niña.
