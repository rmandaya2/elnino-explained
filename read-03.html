What it means for us.

In the Philippines, El Niño usually means less rain than normal. Because the rain clouds have shifted east, many provinces get dry spells and droughts, and these tend to build in the later months of an episode, often overlapping with the dry season.

PAGASA uses two terms to describe how bad it gets. A dry spell is a shorter or milder stretch of below-normal rainfall. A drought is longer and more severe, for example three straight months of rainfall far below normal.

FARMS

Dry fields mean lower harvests, especially for rain-fed rice and corn that depend on rainfall instead of irrigation.

WATER

Dams and reservoirs drop, which can lead to water interruptions in cities and towns.

POWER

Less water for hydropower and more demand for fans and aircon can strain the electricity supply.

HEAT & FIRE

Hotter, drier days raise the risk of heat illness and of grass and forest fires.

WHO'S WATCHING

PAGASA monitors ENSO and announces it in stages. An El Niño Watch means El Niño may develop in the coming months. An El Niño Alert means it is likely soon. An El Niño Advisory means it is already happening. A Final El Niño Advisory says it has ended.

Drier doesn't mean storm-free. Typhoons can still hit, so keep following advisories.
